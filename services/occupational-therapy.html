Common questions

My child has difficulty writing, holding a pencil or using scissors. Can Occupational Therapy help?

Yes. Occupational Therapy can work on fine motor skills, hand strength, coordination, visual-motor skills, handwriting and other skills needed for school and daily activities.

Can therapy help my child become more independent?

Yes. Therapy can focus on practical skills such as dressing, feeding, writing, communication, play, learning, mobility and other daily activities according to the child's needs.

Can my child receive more than one type of therapy?

Yes. Some children benefit from a combination of therapies such as Occupational Therapy, Speech Therapy, Physiotherapy, Behavioral Therapy, Remedial Therapy or Special Education.

How often does my child need therapy?

Therapy frequency depends on the child's needs, goals, age, condition and progress. The therapist will recommend an individualized schedule after assessment.
